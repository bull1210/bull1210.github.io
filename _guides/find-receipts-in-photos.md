You photograph receipts because it is faster than keeping paper. Then the month ends, the expense claim or
the tax return is due, and the receipts are mixed in with the holiday, the kids and four hundred screenshots.
Finding them again is the part nobody planned for.

## The usual ways

- **An album.** Make a "Receipts" album and add each photo to it as you take it. It works if you never forget,
  which means it works for about three weeks.
- **Search "receipt" in Google Photos.** This finds many of them, because Google Photos recognises what a
  receipt looks like and reads its text. It only sees photos that have been backed up, and it does not add
  anything up.
- **A scanner app.** Apps such as Adobe Scan or a bank's expenses app store receipts properly, with totals
  and categories. They need you to open that app at the till instead of the camera, and your old receipts are
  already in the camera roll.
- **A folder.** Some people move receipt photos into a folder with a file manager. It keeps them together,
  but a folder cannot tell you what you spent.

## What it reads and what it misses

The total is read from the text on the receipt, so it depends on the photo. A receipt photographed flat, in
good light, with the total in the frame is read well. A crumpled one at an angle, a faded thermal print, or
one where the total is cut off gives a receipt with no amount; it is still listed and still searchable by
the words that were legible. Handwritten bills are rarely read.

Text is read in the Latin alphabet, so an English or European receipt is fine, and a Tamil or Hindi one is
found by its printed numbers and any English on it, not by its script.

Nothing is categorised for you. The export is the raw list; the sorting into travel, food and office is
yours to do in the spreadsheet, which is usually the part people have opinions about anyway.

## Privacy

A receipt has your card's last digits, the shop, the time and often your name. In PixOrg the recognising and
the reading happen on the phone, and the text stays in PixOrg's own database there. The CSV goes only where
you send it. There is no account and no server. The free version shows small ads under the grid; the ad
network sees an advertising ID, never a photo or a line of a receipt.
