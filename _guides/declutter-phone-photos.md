Most of what fills a phone's gallery was never meant to be kept. The good-morning forward from a group,
the screenshot of a ticket that was used in March, the six shots of the same plate of food, the one that
came out dark. They are not duplicates, so a duplicate finder leaves them, and they are too many to delete
by hand.

The useful question is not "which photos can go" but "which groups of photos can I decide about in one
look". A cleanup that shows you forty expired screenshots together takes a minute. The same forty spread
through three years of photos takes an afternoon nobody has.

## Cleaning up with what the phone has

- **Files by Google** suggests large files, old screenshots and memes under its Clean tab. It works on file
  size and age and does not look at the picture, so a blurry shot or a burst is not found.
- **Google Photos** offers "Free up space", which deletes photos that are already backed up. That empties
  the phone rather than the collection: the forwards are still in your library, just in the cloud.
- **Phone galleries** sometimes group screenshots and have a "Suggested for cleanup" page (Samsung, Xiaomi).
  Check what they consider clutter; some count every photo in a chat folder.

Each of these is worth a few minutes. None of them looks at the photo to decide what it is.

## In PixOrg

Declutter looks at the pictures, groups what it finds by reason, and ticks nothing except exact
duplicates. You review, then delete.

1. Open PixOrg, tap **AI**, then **Declutter**.
2. The page lists groups, biggest first by the space they take:
   - **Forwards**: pictures that arrived through chat apps and were never taken by you (memes, greetings,
     posters).
   - **Expired screenshots**: screenshots old enough that a ticket, a code or a chat has done its job.
   - **Duplicates**: exact copies, the one to keep marked. These are the only photos ticked for you.
   - **Similar shots**: bursts of the same scene. Each row shows the sharpest with a **Keep** badge and the
     rest as copies you can tick.
   - **Blurry or dark**: shots the camera missed.
   - **Old chat media** and **Large videos**: the storage groups, covered in
     [a guide of their own](/guides/free-up-space-old-chat-media/).
3. Open a group. Tick what should go, tap a thumbnail to see it full size, untick anything you want to keep.
4. Tap **Clean up**. Deleted photos go to Android's Bin for 30 days (Android 11 and later).

![PixOrg's Declutter page](/assets/shots/07_declutter.jpg)

Some things PixOrg never suggests, whatever they look like: a photo you rated or made a favourite, a photo
of someone you have named in People, and anything in a folder you made yourself. A dark photo of your
grandmother is a keepsake, not clutter, and the app assumes so.

Every clean-up is written to a review log with **Undo**, so a group deleted by mistake comes back from the
Bin in one tap while the Bin still holds it.

## When to run it

Declutter checks the library after PixOrg has read new photos, and again when the last check is a few
hours old. The AI tab shows the total it has found ("1.2 GB to review") so you can see at a glance whether
a session is worth it. Once a month is plenty for most phones; after a wedding or a trip, the bursts alone
are worth a look.

Exact copies are a job of their own. The [Duplicates tab](/guides/find-duplicate-photos/) finds near copies
too, including the compressed ones chat apps make, and is where to go when a photo seems to be everywhere.

## Privacy

Deciding that a photo is a meme, a burst or a blur means looking at it, and PixOrg does that on the phone
with the models it already uses for search. Nothing is uploaded to decide, and nothing is deleted until you
tap. The free version shows a small ad while a check runs; the ad network never sees a photo.
