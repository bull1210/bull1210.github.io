You want four photos in one picture for a chat or a print. Every collage app offers the same thing: a grid
of equal cells, and your photos stretched or cut to fill them. The tall photo of the kids loses its heads,
the wide one of the beach loses its sky, and the app offers "pinch to adjust" as if moving the crop around
were a fix.

The problem is the fixed cell. A photo is 4:3 or 3:4 or something in between, and a square cell can only
show part of it.

## The usual ways

- **Google Photos** has a collage tool with a few templates. Photos are cropped to the cells; you can drag
  inside a cell to choose which part survives.
- **Collage apps** (PhotoGrid, Canva, Layout) have hundreds of layouts, all with fixed cells, plus
  stickers and a watermark on the free version. Some offer a "fit" mode that leaves a photo whole inside
  its cell with empty bands around it, which looks unfinished.
- **Samsung Gallery** makes a collage from selected photos with a handful of layouts, cropped to fit.

If cropping is fine for your photos, any of these is quick. If a photo must be shown whole, none of them
quite does it.

## In PixOrg

PixOrg's collage layouts are made of rows and columns, and the lines between them move so that each cell
has exactly the shape of its photo. A photo is never cropped; the layout bends to the photos.

1. Long-press a photo in any grid, tick the others (two to about nine work best), and tap **Collage** in
   the action bar.
2. The preview appears at once. Swipe along the row of layouts to see the same photos arranged differently;
   each layout is drawn with your real photos, whole.
3. Choose the **shape**: Square, Portrait or Story (the tall one for status posts).
4. Choose the **border**: White, Black, or None. With None, the space the photos do not fill is a soft,
   darkened copy of the first photo, so there is no blank band.
5. Tap **Save**. The collage is written as a new photo in your gallery. The originals are untouched.

The order you ticked the photos is the order they take in the layout, reading left to right and top to
bottom, so tick the photo you want largest first in layouts that have a hero cell.

Why the photos stay whole: a layout here is only a set of rows and columns. When you add your photos,
PixOrg works out where the lines must go so that every cell matches its photo's own proportions, swaps two
photos when that fills the picture better, and turns the whole layout on its side if that uses the space
better. What is left over becomes an even margin. One layout, the staggered four that is the Collage icon,
is allowed to trim a little from each edge so that the stagger always shows; every other layout shows every
pixel.

## A PDF instead of a collage

For a form, a set of receipts or a few pages of a document, a collage is the wrong shape. Tick the photos
and choose **PDF** in the same action bar: each photo becomes one page, in the order you ticked them, saved
as a PDF you can send. Receipts have their own home in PixOrg too, see
[finding receipts in your photos](/guides/find-receipts-in-photos/).

There is also **Resize**, which saves a smaller copy of each ticked photo when a form or a site wants a
file under a certain size. Like the collage, it is always a copy; the original stays.

## Privacy

The collage is drawn on the phone and saved next to your other photos. Nothing is uploaded, and the free
version shows a small ad on the sheet while it works, which sees neither your photos nor the result.
